# Students whose mother tongue is not Spanish in Peru (EIB): teaching, assessment, language technology and adaptive assessment (to 2026)

Research note: about 18 searches and fetches. Two primary PDFs could not be fetched: the Congreso MINEDU deck from April 2025 (DNS failure) and the official ENLA 2023 presentation (file too large). Figures attributed to them come from search-result extracts and should be checked against the original PDFs.

## 1. Numbers: EIB students, schools, languages, forms of service

### Takeaway
Peru's EIB system has about 26,400–26,600 schools (IIEE) and about 1.2 million students, and serves 45 of the 48 lenguas originarias, with 106,000 EIB teachers. I could not obtain a current breakdown by language, region or form of service (fortalecimiento, revitalización, urbano) from accessible sources.

### Cited Findings
- April 2025: the Registro Nacional de IIEE EIB listed 26,564 EIB schools (PRONOEI included) out of 83,302 EBR schools, which is about 32%. About 1.23 million students study in some lengua originaria. This comes from MINEDU's presentation to the Congreso Comisión de Pueblos Andinos (08-04-2025), via a search extract; the PDF was not fetched directly — [MINEDU deck, Congreso](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/comisiones2024/PueblosAndinos/files/so-13-exposiciÓn-02-minedu-08-04-2025.pdf)
- November 2025: 26,422 EIB schools, 1,203,697 students and 106,039 EIB teachers. MINEDU serves 45 of the 48 registered lenguas originarias and 12 of the 17 languages at risk of disappearing. Pedagogical materials exist in 42 lenguas originarias plus 6 Quechua varieties — [El Búho, Nov 2025](https://elbuho.pe/2025/11/minedu-atiende-a-estudiantes-en-12-lenguas-originarias-en-riesgo-de-desaparicion/)
- Enrollment in the at-risk languages runs from 79 students (Maijiki) to 33,380 (Quechua wanka). The 12 at-risk languages served are Maijiki, Arabela, Ikitu, Amahuaca, Ese eja, Bora, Kawki, Harakbut, Shiwilu, Kapanawa, Murui muinani and Quechua wanka — [El Búho](https://elbuho.pe/2025/11/minedu-atiende-a-estudiantes-en-12-lenguas-originarias-en-riesgo-de-desaparicion/)
- July 2026: still "more than 26,000" EIB schools and "more than 1,200,000" students — [El Peruano, July 2026](https://www.elperuano.pe/noticia/289827-mejor-ensenanza-en-zonas-rurales)
- A MINEDU deck to the Congreso Comisión de Educación (2022 filename) gives a table by form of service: fortalecimiento 481, revitalización 459, ámbitos urbanos 313, total 1,253. It is unclear what the unit is (schools or teaching posts). This cannot be the full school count, because it is far below 26,000 — [MINEDU ppt 22-10, Congreso](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/comisiones2022/Educacion/files/ppt_minedu_22-10.pdf)

### Inferences
- About 1 in 3 public EBR schools is EIB. For a tool, the long tail matters: dozens of languages have only hundreds of students, and almost no digital resources exist for them.
- 6 Quechua varieties have separate materials, so "Quechua" cannot be treated as one language for content or speech technology. At minimum, Collao, Chanka, central/Wanka and others need separate handling.

### Gaps
- Per-language and per-region student counts for Quechua Collao, Chanka, Aimara, Asháninka, Awajún and Shipibo. These are in ESCALE or the RNIIEE-EIB downloads, which were not fetched.
- The official distribution of schools by form of service (EIB de fortalecimiento vs revitalización vs urbano). The 481/459/313 figures are of uncertain meaning.

## 2. How EIB students are assessed (ECE/ENLA, Castellano L2, teacher proficiency) and the problems

### Takeaway
UMC has run 4° primaria EIB reading assessments: ECE 2012, 2016 and 2018, then ENLA 2023. They cover reading in a few lenguas originarias (Quechua Cusco-Collao, Quechua Chanka, Aimara, Awajún, Shipibo-Konibo, Asháninka) and in Castellano como segunda lengua. Results are very low and fell between 2016 and 2018. Teachers' language proficiency is certified through EDLO.

### Cited Findings
- ECE 2012 assessed 4° primaria EIB students in reading in Castellano L2 and in 4 lenguas originarias: Awajún, Quechua Cusco-Collao, Aimara and Shipibo-Conibo — [MINEDU repositorio ECE 2012](https://repositorio.minedu.gob.pe/handle/20.500.12799/3933)
- ECE 2016 covered Aimara, Awajún, Quechua Cusco-Collao, Quechua Chanka, Shipibo-Konibo and Asháninka, plus Castellano L2 — [MINEDU repositorio ECE 2016](https://repositorio.minedu.gob.pe/handle/20.500.12799/9264)
- ECE 2018, Castellano L2:
  - All EIB students at "satisfactorio" fell from 25.1% (2016) to 10.4% (2018).
  - Aimara students fell from 49.7% to 26.3%.
  - Shipibo-Konibo students fell from 9.5% to 6.9%.
  - Quechua Cusco students "en inicio" rose from 24.6% to 45.7%.
  — [RPP](https://rpp.pe/peru/actualidad/solo-10-de-estudiantes-de-escuelas-interculturales-bilingues-comprenden-lo-que-leen-noticia-1191896)
- ECE 2018, lengua originaria: Aimara students at "satisfactorio" in reading went from 10.2% (2016) to 0% (2018) — [RPP](https://rpp.pe/peru/actualidad/solo-10-de-estudiantes-de-escuelas-interculturales-bilingues-comprenden-lo-que-leen-noticia-1191896)
- Implementation context:
  - Only 6.8% of UGELs implement EIB pedagogical-management models, according to Plan EIB al 2021.
  - A UARM EIB coordinator (Óscar Marín) says local communities do not know what intercultural education means.
  — [RPP](https://rpp.pe/peru/actualidad/solo-10-de-estudiantes-de-escuelas-interculturales-bilingues-comprenden-lo-que-leen-noticia-1191896)
- ENLA 2023 assessed reading in Castellano L2 in 4° primaria EIB with 24,120 students in 2,572 schools, reported by achievement levels (search extract of the official presentation) — [ENLA 2023 presentation (Lampadia mirror)](https://www.lampadia.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Presentacion_de_logros_de_aprendizaje_ENLA_2023.pdf)
- INEI recruited test administrators for "Primaria 4° EIB L2 y L1" for ENLA 2023 in Ayacucho, Cusco, Junín, Ucayali, Apurímac, Puno, San Martín and Amazonas, which suggests an L1 component was also given — [INEI convocatoria](https://www.perutrabajos.com/empleos-para-aplicador-inei-octubre-2023-54908.html)
- A 2024 MINEDU study of factors associated with Castellano L2 reading in EIB schools, using Estudio EIB 2022 data, found notable differences between Andean-language and Amazonian-language schools — [MINEDU repositorio](https://repositorio.minedu.gob.pe/handle/20.500.12799/10366)
- Teacher proficiency, EDLO (Evaluación de Dominio de la Lengua Indígena u Originaria):
  - RM 331-2025-MINEDU created the Registro de Docentes Bilingües and its technical norm — [El Peruano](https://busquedas.elperuano.pe/dispositivo/NL/2474429-1)
  - EDLO 2026 invited more than 90,000 teachers, with 100% virtual registration via PerúEduca until 23 March 2026 — [El Peruano](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/290399-minedu-convoca-a-mas-de-90-000-docentes-a-evaluacion-de-dominio-de-la-lengua-originaria)
  - The EDLO 2026 protocol has three participant groups: new applicants, teachers whose certification expires, and teachers seeking a higher level — [Protocolo EDLO 2026](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/9483831/7764755-protocolo-general-de-la-evaluacion-de-dominio-de-la-lengua-indigena-u-originaria-edlo-2026.pdf)
  - In EDLO 2025, 7,982 assessed teachers were not registered because they lacked a professional teaching degree — [Oficio Múltiple 00018-2026-MINEDU/VMGP-DIGEIBIRA](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/9478769/7760841-oficio_multiple-00018-2026-minedu-vmgp-digeibira-elavuacion-excepcional.pdf)
  - In 2019, more than 70,000 teachers were assessed, and MINEDU said bilingual teachers needed to reach an advanced level — [Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-mas-70000-docentes-fueron-evaluados-lenguas-originarias-755262.aspx)
  - In 2024 an extraordinary EDLO for higher-pedagogical teachers assessed oral and written skills — [MINEDU superior pedagógica](https://www.minedu.gob.pe/superiorpedagogica/?p=17153)

### Inferences
- National L1 assessment covers only about 6 of 48 languages, and only for written reading. Most EIB students therefore have no standardized measure in their own language. That is a real gap a tool could fill, with oral items first.
- The 0% Aimara L1 result alongside a 26% Aimara L2 result suggests children are not being taught to read in their L1 (written-tradition gap), or that the L1 test was misaligned (dialect or orthography). Both possibilities argue for oral-first measurement and local norming.
- EDLO measures both oral and written skills, which confirms that written proficiency even among teachers is the bottleneck.

### Gaps
- ENLA 2023 percentages by level, for both L2 and L1, broken down by language. The official PDF could not be fetched.
- UMC technical documents on translation vs adaptation of items, and on dialect handling (for example Chanka vs Collao versions). No accessible source was found in this pass.
- Aggregate EDLO results: the share of EIB teachers at basic, intermediate and advanced levels.

## 3. Pedagogical evidence: mother tongue first, oral vs written, L2 testing bias

### Takeaway
The World Bank's "Loud and Clear" (2021) finds that children learn more, and are likelier to stay in school, when first taught in a language they speak. About 37% of LMIC students learn in a language that is not their own, and they are concentrated in the poorest 40%. Phone and oral assessment guidance warns that instructions must be simplified so that tests measure the skill and not language comprehension.

### Cited Findings
- Children learn more and are likelier to stay in school when first taught in a language they speak and understand — [World Bank press release, 14 July 2021](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/07/14/teaching-young-children-in-the-language-they-speak-at-home-is-essential-to-eliminate-learning-poverty)
- About 37% of LMIC students are taught in a language they do not understand. Those affected are disproportionately in the bottom 40% and in remote areas, and the policy is linked to higher dropout and repetition. Large gains are possible by adding a small number of languages — [World Bank, Loud and Clear](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/education/publication/loud-and-clear-effective-language-of-instruction-policies-for-learning); [OKR full text](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/537645d3-981c-5137-836d-f7cfd8ac46d6/full)
- Phone-based assessments should simplify instructions and practice items so that the test measures the target skill rather than general language or test-taking ability — [Practical lessons for phone-based assessments, BMJ Global Health / PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7380711/)
- The IDB has an experimental study of mobile learning of Indigenous languages in a Peruvian school intervention (content not reviewed) — [IDB](https://publications.iadb.org/publications/spanish/document/Aprendizaje-movil-de-lenguas-indigenas-evidencia-experimental-de-una-intervencion-escolar-en-Peru.pdf)

### Inferences
- Testing EIB children on reasoning or critical thinking in Castellano confounds L2 proficiency with the construct being measured. The tool should let a child answer in L1, orally, and score the construct separately from Spanish proficiency.

### Gaps
- Specific UNESCO 2024–2025 effect sizes (for example the GEM "If you don't understand, how can you learn?" materials) and Peru-specific causal estimates of EIB effects were not retrieved.
- Direct evidence comparing oral and written assessment validity for Andean and Amazonian children was not found in this pass.

## 4. Language technology for Quechua, Aimara and Amazonian languages

### Takeaway
Google Translate has supported Quechua and Aymara since 2022 (zero-shot MT). NLLB-200 supports Quechua and Aymara, and fine-tuning helps. AmericasNLP 2025 systems reach only about chrF++ 31 for Spanish-Quechua. Frontier LLMs were reported at BLEU about 1.5 on Quechua translation, against 17.6 for a fine-tuned RAG system. Quechua ASR exists in research form, for example Siminchik (97 hours of speech, about 12.7% WER). Amazonian languages are barely covered. Human-in-the-loop review by native speakers is mandatory.

### Cited Findings
- Google Translate added 24 languages in May 2022, including Quechua and Aymara, using zero-shot MT with native-speaker and linguist collaborators. No evidence was found of Shipibo, Asháninka or Awajún being added — [Andina](https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-google-translate-adds-24-new-languages-including-quechua-and-aymara-892736.aspx); [AP via TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2022-05-google-multilingual-nuance.pdf)
- Peru's Ministerio de Cultura publicly welcomed the Google Translate inclusion — [Andina](https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-ministerio-cultura-destaca-incorporacion-lenguas-indigenas-traductor-google-893018.aspx)
- The Ministerio de Cultura worked with Microsoft to relaunch Office in Quechua (2022) — [RPP](https://rpp.pe/tecnologia/mas-tecnologia/ministerio-de-cultura-trabaja-con-microsoft-para-relanzar-una-version-de-office-en-quechua-noticia-1405369)
- No official government AI translator for lenguas originarias was found as of 2025–2026. The Ministerio de Cultura relies on human translators, for example for the 2025 official slogan in 38 languages — [Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-traducen-a-38-lenguas-nativas-frase-ano-recuperacion-y-consolidacion-de-economia-1031133.aspx)
- AmericasNLP 2025 Shared Task 1: MT for Indigenous languages of the Americas — [AmericasNLP 2025 ST1](https://turing.iimas.unam.mx/americasnlp/2025_st_1.html)
  - Fine-tuned NLLB-200 600M, Llama 3.1 8B and XGLM across 13 languages reached chrF++ 31.01 (es→quy) and 31.71 (quy→es), with relatively good results for Awajún and Quechua — [ACL Anthology 2025.americasnlp-1.15](https://preview.aclanthology.org/watermark/2025.americasnlp-1.15)
  - UCSP (Arequipa) used LLM-generated synthetic Quechua sentences and mT5 fine-tuning to improve BLEU and chrF++ — [ACL Anthology 2025.americasnlp-1.9](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2025.americasnlp-1.9)
- The Sheffield AmericasNLP 2023 entry, built on NLLB-200, improved chrF by 11% on average and gained most for Aymara, Guarani and Quechua. NLLB is "not optimised" for these languages — [arXiv 2306.09830](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.09830v2)
- IWSLT 2026: NLLB-200 retrained for Quechua–Spanish on 22,891 curated parallel pairs, with BLEU gains — [ACL Anthology 2026.iwslt-1.35](https://aclanthology.org/2026.iwslt-1.35/)
- QueEn (December 2024) used RAG plus LoRA for Quechua–English and reached BLEU 17.6, against 1.5 for standard GPT models. Slator names GPT-4o and Llama 405B as the baselines — [arXiv 2412.05184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05184v1); [Slator](https://tech.slator.com/?p=101837)
- SeamlessM4T is built on NLLB and supports speech recognition, speech translation and text translation. Meta's MMS covers ASR, TTS and language ID for more than 1,100 languages. Per-language coverage for Quechua, Aymara and Asháninka was not verified — [Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/08/seamlessm4t-ai-translation-model/)
- Siminchik is the first Southern Quechua speech corpus: 97 hours of radio conversations from southern Peru, annotated by native speakers in the unified orthography. An HTK ASR built on it reached 12.70% WER on volunteer recordings. The HUQARIQ app was tested, but not at scale — [PUCP repositorio](https://repositorio.pucp.edu.pe/items/e75bafd0-08fe-4350-8eb9-6ad621f2bdef/full)
- A PUCP thesis notes that Southern Quechua corpora are still too small for quality translators — [PUCP tesis](https://tesis.pucp.edu.pe/repositorio/handle/20.500.12404/23751)
- A non-peer-reviewed 2026 blog says nearly all Indigenous languages fall well below top-tier LLM performance, and many have never been measured — [capitalandcompute.net](https://capitalandcompute.net/md/which-languages-do-llms-support-well.md)

### Inferences
- Machine output in Quechua and Aimara (chrF++ around 30) is useful for drafting but not for unreviewed assessment items. Items must be written or adapted by native-speaker teachers, ideally EDLO-certified ones. MT or LLMs can serve as drafting aids, with per-variant review.
- For Amazonian languages (Asháninka, Awajún, Shipibo, and the at-risk languages), assume no reliable MT, ASR or TTS. Use human-recorded audio prompts and human scoring of child audio.
- Quechua ASR at about 12.7% WER on adult speech will be worse on children's speech and other variants. Automated oral-reading scoring is not ready, so human-in-the-loop scoring is required.

### Gaps
- Hinantin (PUCP) tools, the Siminchik license and availability, and Common Voice Quechua hours were not retrieved.
- No 2025–2026 peer-reviewed benchmark of frontier LLMs (GPT-5-class, Claude, Gemini) on Quechua comprehension was found.
- MMS and Seamless per-language support for Asháninka, Awajún and Shipibo is unverified.

## 5. Adaptive assessment: CAT/IRT, adaptive learning in LMICs, oral and phone assessment, reading-fluency tools

### Takeaway
Adaptive software works in LMICs: Mindspark in Delhi gave ITT +0.36σ in math and +0.22σ in Hindi after 4.5 months, and IV +0.59σ and +0.36σ for 90 days of attendance. TaRL is low-tech adaptivity with some of the largest effects in the literature, but only with mentoring and data use. Phone-based ASER-style assessment gave distributions similar to face-to-face assessment in Botswana, for numeracy only, at about US$4.40 per child. AI oral-reading-fluency tools (Google Read Along, Amira) exist for Spanish but not for Peruvian lenguas originarias.

### Cited Findings
- Mindspark (Muralidharan, Singh, Ganimian), RCT with 619 students in grades 6–9 in Delhi:
  - ITT +0.36σ in math and +0.22σ in Hindi after 4.5 months.
  - IV for 90 days of attendance: +0.59σ in math and +0.36σ in Hindi.
  - Relative gains were largest for the weakest students.
  — [NBER w22923 summary](https://causalclaims.trfetzer.com/paper/w22923.html); [RISE blog](https://riseprogramme.org/blog/delhi_experiment_sparks_success.html); [UCSD, AER 2019](https://economics.ucsd.edu/events/news/MuralidharanAER2019.html)
- Note: the brief's "0.37–0.59 SD" roughly matches these figures. The exact numbers vary between the working paper and the AER version.
- TaRL:
  - 6 randomized evaluations across 7 Indian states show consistent effects, among the largest rigorously measured in education. Teacher training or materials alone did not work; clear goals, child-level data and on-site mentoring did — [J-PAL TaRL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/scale-ups/teaching-right-level)
  - Zambia scaled TaRL to 227,000 learners in 1,800 schools, and 10 other African countries run it — [J-PAL Africa](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/fr/node/2060924?lang=fr)
- Phone assessment in Botswana:
  - More than 2,000 children in grades 3–5, phone-based ASER-style numeracy compared with a face-to-face sample, gave broadly similar skill distributions at about US$4.40 per child — [CGD blog](https://cgdev.org/blog/potential-assessing-childrens-learning-phone-update)
  - The evidence is preliminary and covers numeracy only. The authors recommend validating reliability, and they found no prior published phone-administered child learning assessments — [PMC7380711](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7380711/); [CGD principles](https://cgdev.org/sites/default/files/principles-phone-based-assessments-learning.pdf)
- Google Read Along:
  - Supports American English, Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese, and gives teachers per-student accuracy, fluency and comprehension data — [Google support](https://support.google.com/edu/classroom/answer/14174515?hl=en)
  - Stories can be downloaded for offline use (secondary source) — [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/google-bolo)
- Amira en español measures oral reading fluency, decoding and dyslexia risk, based on the Tejas Lee assessment. It is US-market — [Amira](https://amiralearning.com/spanish.html)

### Inferences
- Design implication: use a TaRL-style coarse placement (levels such as letter, word, paragraph or story, or ASER levels) delivered orally in L1 by phone or WhatsApp voice. Within levels, use IRT-calibrated item banks and keep separate calibrations per language and variant, because items translated across Quechua variants may not be parameter-invariant.
- Castellano L2 oral-reading fluency could be bootstrapped with Spanish ASR or Read Along-style tools. L1 fluency needs human raters, such as EDLO-certified teachers, until ASR improves.
- CAT basics (IRT item calibration, ability estimation, maximum-information item selection, stopping rules) are standard. No peer-reviewed source was fetched in this pass, so the report should cite a psychometrics reference separately.

### Gaps
- Youth Impact's own phone assessment publications were not retrieved, and the CGD and Botswana work is the closest found.
- onebillion (onecourse) RCT effect sizes were not found in this pass.
- No tool was found that assesses oral reading in Quechua, Aimara or Amazonian languages via WhatsApp voice notes. This appears to be an open niche, which I infer from absence and have not confirmed.
- No evidence was found of CAT being used in any Peruvian national or EIB assessment.
