# How critical thinking is assessed in school students, by developmental stage, and which ages to target (to 2026)

Scope note: about 16 tool calls, run fast. Search-engine summaries were used for several items. Wherever a summary was the only source, the claim is labeled. Older (pre-2015) sources are labeled [older].

## 1. Standard instruments: age ranges, formats, validity concerns

### Takeaway
The best-known critical thinking (CT) tests are multiple-choice instruments built for adults or university students: Watson-Glaser, the CCTST, and Cornell Level Z. The main one usable with school-age children is **Cornell Level X**, a 71-item multiple-choice test for grades 5–12+. Its psychometrics are weak once translated. Assessments of CT in real-world digital settings are increasingly **constructed-response performance tasks** scored with short rubrics (SHEG/COR uses Beginning/Emerging/Mastery). For a writing-based tool, this is the closest model.

### Cited Findings
- **Cornell Critical Thinking Test Level X** is a 71-item multiple-choice test for **grades 5–12+**. It runs 50 minutes timed or can be untimed, and covers induction, deduction, credibility and identification of assumptions. **Level Z** has 52 items and is for advanced high school, college and adult test-takers. — [Cornell CCTT publisher sheet (WVU-hosted PDF)](https://health.wvu.edu/media/15198/cornell-critical-thinking-tests.pdf)
- A Spanish-language paper describes Level X as covering **grades 4–14**, which conflicts with the publisher's 5–12+. — [Anales de Psicología (revistas.um.es)](https://revistas.um.es/analesps/article/download/analesps.34.2.272401/227441)
- Translated Level X showed weak internal consistency (KR-20 = 0.76 overall, subtests 0.45–0.63). Its original factor structure was not confirmed (Turkish study). — [YÖK Açık Bilim thesis](https://acikbilim.yok.gov.tr/handle/20.500.12812/221015)
- A cross-cultural dissertation found Level X items behaved similarly for Turkish and US students after translation review. It still cautioned against comparing scores across cultures because the factor structures differ. — [WSU dissertation](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/Cross-Cultural-Validation-of-the-Cornell-Critical/99900581823401842)
- A 2013 Dutch study compared translated Cornell and Halpern (HCTA) tests [older]. HCTA had higher content validity and students preferred it, while Cornell was quicker to give and score. Neither showed high reliability. — [Wiley/Hindawi 2013 article 198920](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/10.1155/2013/198920) (proxy URL, from a search summary)
- Halpern's everyday-situations test uses a **double response format**: an open (constructed) answer followed by a closed (forced-choice) one. A Spanish validation used 335 students, mostly university students. — [REMA, Univ. Oviedo](https://reunido.uniovi.es/index.php/Rema/article/download/9787/9526/16189)
- The **CCTST** (Insight Assessment) is aimed at undergraduate and graduate students. It has 34 multiple-choice items and takes about 45 minutes. It covers interpretation, inference, evaluation, explanation, induction, deduction and numeracy, with score bands from "Not Manifested" to "Superior". — [Insight Assessment CCTST page](https://insightassessment.com/iaproduct/california-critical-thinking-skills-test/); [EdInstruments CCTST entry](https://edinstruments.org/node/533); [MTSU interpretation guide](https://iepr.mtsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/23/2024/09/cctstinterpret.pdf)
- **Facione / APA Delphi consensus (1990)** [older but foundational]. A panel of 46 experts defined CT with a **skills** dimension and a **dispositions** dimension. CCTST and CCTDI are built on this definition. — [Salisbury Univ. CCTDI/CCTST report](https://wwwnew.salisbury.edu/administration/university-analysis-reporting-and-assessment/_files/2016-12-21_FA15SP16-GULLWeek_CCTDICCTST_CriticalThinkingInterdependenceAmongDisciplinesIntellectualCuriosity_Report_v4-1.pdf); [Buros review of CCTST](https://marketplace.unl.edu/buros/test-reviews/alphabetical-list/california-critical-thinking-skills-test-revised.html)
- **SHEG / Digital Inquiry Group, Civic Online Reasoning (COR)**. The 2019 national study gave 3,446 US high school students in 16 districts across 14 states six constructed-response web tasks, fielded June 2018 to May 2019. Responses were rated Beginning, Emerging or Mastery. Fewer than 2% were rated Mastery and more than 96% were rated Beginning. 96% never noticed a climate site's fossil-fuel funding, about two-thirds could not tell news from ads, and 90% got no credit on four of the six tasks. — [Stanford PURL report](https://purl.stanford.edu/cz440cm8408); [Stanford News 2019](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2019/11/high-school-students-unequipped-spot-fake-news)
- **PISA 2022 Creative Thinking** was first assessed in 2022 with 15-year-olds and scored out of 60. Peru scored 23 against an OECD average of 33. Brazil also scored 23, Chile 31, Mexico and Uruguay 29, Colombia 26, and Singapore led with 41. — [OECD PISA 2022 Vol. III Peru factsheet](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-results-2022-volume-iii-factsheets_041a90f1-en/peru_fb5a37a1-en.html); [CUHK annex of country scores](https://cpr.cuhk.edu.hk/wp-content/uploads/newscentre/pressrelease/20240618-PISA-2022-Creative-Thinking_Annex_Eng.pdf); [La República 2024](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2024/06/18/peru-obtuvo-bajo-puntaje-en-pensamiento-creativo-1237194)
- **PISA 2025 Learning in the Digital World (LDW)** is defined as an iterative, self-regulated process of building knowledge and solving problems with computational tools. It has two strands: computational problem solving and **self-regulated learning**. SRL covers control of metacognition, cognition, motivation, affect and behavior, with monitoring and evaluating inside the loop. Students choose how long to spend on subtasks and can use tutorials, worked examples and adaptive feedback. ACER developed it. The framework document found is the second draft from October 2023. — [OECD LDW framework, 2nd draft](https://www.oecd.org/media/oecdorg/satellitesites/pisa/PISA%202025%20Learning%20in%20the%20Digital%20World%20Assessment%20Framework%20-%20Second%20Draft.pdf); [Scottish Government PISA 2025 report](https://www.gov.scot/publications/pisa-2025-national-report-scotland/pages/8/); [TUM PISA page](https://www.pisa.tum.de/en/pisa/kompetenzbereiche/lernen-in-der-digitalen-welt/); [DIPF PISA-LDW](https://tba.dipf.de/en/projects/pisa-ldw-assessing-competencies-for-learning-in-the-digital-world)
- A Hong Kong presentation states that LDW SRL results were scheduled for release in 2027, separately from the computational problem-solving scale. — [HKU PISA 2025 presentation](https://web.edu.hku.hk/f/news/52990/PISA2025_Presentation%20File_English.pdf)

### Inferences
- Validated multiple-choice CT tests mostly fit adults. For ages 11–14 the realistic options are Cornell X or performance tasks, and Cornell X loses validity in translation. A Peruvian tool would therefore need its own validation in any case.
- The SHEG COR format (short constructed response plus a 3-level rubric) is the closest validated analogue to scoring a student's written answer on WhatsApp.
- The PISA LDW SRL strand and PISA Creative Thinking give internationally recognised construct definitions for 15-year-olds, with Peru baseline data (creative thinking 23/60).

### Gaps
- Watson-Glaser official age range was not checked; it is typically 16+/adult, unverified here.
- The Ennis-Weir essay test (an essay format) and the HCTA's official age range were not found in this pass.
- No child version of CCTST/CCTDI was confirmed. Insight Assessment markets K-12 products (e.g. "INSIGHT Junior", "CCTST M-series"), but grades could not be verified. "CCT-G835" could not be verified at all and may be a mistaken name.
- AAC&U VALUE rubric and Paul-Elder framework were not fetched. Both are widely known as higher-education or general rubrics. VALUE has 5 dimensions (explanation of issues; evidence; context and assumptions; student's position; conclusions) on a 4-level scale, but this is from training knowledge and not verified here.

## 2. Rubric-based and performance assessment for K-12 (OECD-CERI, PBL)

### Takeaway
The OECD-CERI project *Fostering Students' Creativity and Critical Thinking* (2019) trialled rubrics and lesson plans with teacher networks in 11 countries, **including Brazil**, at primary and secondary level, with control groups. Its rubrics are the most relevant international, multi-country, school-age reference. The detailed outcome numbers were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- The OECD worked with school and teacher networks in 11 countries to develop and trial pedagogical resources (rubrics and lesson plans) for primary and secondary education. Published October 2019, edited by Stéphan Vincent-Lancrin et al. — [OECD publication page](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/fostering-students-creativity-and-critical-thinking_62212c37-en); [OECD iLibrary](https://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/education/fostering-students-creativity-and-critical-thinking_62212c37-en)
- Participants included Brazil, France, Hungary, India, the Netherlands, Russia, Slovakia, Spain and Thailand, in primary and secondary education. — [OECD project page](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/projects/teaching-learning-and-assessing-creative-and-critical-thinking-skills.html)
- Methods included questionnaires given to intervention and control groups, plus interviews, focus groups and observations. One chapter covers effects on student outcomes, validation of survey instruments, and a class-level analysis of the most successful classes. Thailand assigned schools to groups by stratified random sampling, with data from November 2015 to May 2017. — [OECD iLibrary chapter listing](https://oecd-ilibrary.org/education/fostering-students-creativity-and-critical-thinking_39557c9f-en); [OECD CERI 2015 project document](https://one.oecd.org/document/EDU/CERI/CD(2015)12/en/pdf)
- The OECD later built a higher-education version of the same rubric approach. — [OECD CERI HE project](https://www.oecd.org/education/ceri/fostering-assessing-students-creative-and-critical-thinking-skills-in-higher-education.htm)

### Inferences
- The OECD-CERI critical-thinking rubric's four dimensions are commonly cited as *inquiring, imagining, doing, reflecting* (from training knowledge, not verified in this pass). They map directly onto steps in a "written process" and are a strong candidate scoring frame. Brazil's participation supports relevance for Latin America.

### Gaps
- The OECD page returned HTTP 403, so effect sizes, rubric text and age-band descriptors were not retrieved.
- Buck Institute (PBLWorks) critical thinking rubrics, available in grades K-2, 3-5, 6-12 bands, were not researched in this pass.

## 3. Developmental evidence: metacognition, reasoning, intervention effects by age

### Takeaway
Metacognition is not switched on at age 8–10. Monitoring and control develop slowly and separately. Retrospective monitoring and self-regulation improve clearly from middle childhood into adolescence, and **metacognitive accuracy keeps improving from 11 to 17**. CT instruction works at all school levels with a small-to-moderate average effect (g ≈ 0.30). Combining authentic problems, dialogue and mentoring works best (g ≈ 0.57). The UK Philosophy for Children (P4C) result for ages 9–10 (+2 months) **did not replicate** in the larger EEF effectiveness trial.

### Cited Findings
- The idea that metacognitive skills emerge around ages 8–10 is now challenged. Observational studies find metacognitive and self-regulatory behaviour in younger children, and self-report or verbal measures may underestimate young children's metacognition. — [Leiden thesis chapter](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A2936912/view); [Springer chapter, Metacognition development](https://mijn.bsl.nl/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4419-6546-2_11) (both from a search summary)
- O'Leary & Sloutsky (2016/2017, *Child Development*, 88(3):1015–1032). 5-year-olds did not spontaneously monitor or control effort, 7-year-olds were transitional, and adults did both. Monitoring and control are separate processes, both slow to develop. Explicit strategy instruction improved control even in 5-year-olds, so the problem is spontaneous use rather than missing ability. The authors link this to prefrontal and working-memory maturation continuing into early adolescence. — [PMC5397377](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5397377)
- Bayard et al. (2024) studied 7/8- and 9/10-year-olds. Retrospective monitoring improved with age, but prospective monitoring and control did not, and control stayed suboptimal because of overconfident monitoring. — [Univ. Bern / Child Development PDF](https://boris.unibe.ch/160923/8/cdev.13486.pdf) (from a search summary)
- Schneider's metamemory review: declarative metacognitive knowledge grows into adolescence, and self-regulation gains clearly from middle childhood to adolescence. — [Augsburg OPUS PDF](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/deliver/index/docId/104336/file/104336.pdf) (attribution from a search summary)
- Weil et al. (2013, *Consciousness and Cognition* 22(1):264–271) [older] tested 56 people aged 11–41. Metacognitive ability improved significantly during adolescence and peaked in late adolescence, while task performance stayed stable. The 11–17 gain reflects better self-assessment. — [PMC3719211](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3719211)
- **Abrami et al. (2015), *Review of Educational Research* 85(2):275–314.** 341 effect sizes from quasi- and true-experimental studies using standardized CT measures gave a random-effects mean of **g+ = 0.30** (range −1 to +2), with significant heterogeneity. The strongest effect, **g = 0.57**, came from combining authentic or applied problems, dialogue and mentoring. CT skills and dispositions can be taught "at all educational levels and across all disciplinary areas." — [TUM Clearinghouse summary](https://www.clearinghouse.edu.tum.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/CHU_KR18_ENG_Abrami_2015.pdf); [Stanford Encyclopedia supplement](https://plato.stanford.edu/eNtRIeS/critical-thinking/methods.html)
- Abrami et al. (2008, RER) [older] covered 117 studies and 20,698 participants, giving 161 effects with mean **g+ = 0.341**. Intervention type and pedagogical grounding explained 32% of the variance. — [SAGE RER 2008](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654308326084)
- **EEF P4C efficacy trial** (Durham, report July 2015): 3,159 pupils in 48 schools, Years 4–5 (ages 9–10). Pupils gained about **+2 months** in KS2 reading and maths. Disadvantaged pupils gained +4 months in reading, +3 in maths and +2 in writing. Cost was under £30 per pupil. The evidence was rated moderate security, and the methodology was publicly criticised. — [EEF report (DERA)](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk//32011/1/EEF_Project_Report_PhilosophyForChildren.pdf); [EEF project page](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/philosophy-for-children); [Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/philosophy-sessions-improve-pupil-learning)
- **EEF P4C effectiveness trial** (larger scale): FSM-eligible pupils made no additional reading progress (the primary outcome), and there was no evidence of impact on reading or maths for all pupils. — [EEF effectiveness trial page](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/philosophy-for-children-effectiveness-trial)
- **SHEG COR, middle school.** In one class, 3 of 87 students read laterally before the course and 67 of 87 after it. The program is six one-hour lessons, and a randomized trial in an urban district was published in the *Journal of Educational Psychology*. A separate University of Florida 90-minute intervention with 8th graders raised "open new tab and Google the site" behaviour. — [DIG COR blog: middle school](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/blog/teaching-middle-school-students); [Stanford GSE news](https://ed-old.gse.stanford.edu/news/it-doesn-t-take-long-learn-how-spot-misinformation-online-stanford-study-finds)

### Inferences
- Ages 11–14 sit where explicit metacognitive knowledge is present, self-regulation is improving fast, and metacognitive accuracy is still developing. Measured gains are therefore likely there, and short explicit instruction (SHEG) produces visible behaviour change.
- Below ages 9–10, written self-report of reasoning probably underestimates ability (verbal-method bias). A writing or chat-based tool is less valid for young children.
- The P4C non-replication is a warning against promising attainment gains. Frame the tool as measuring CT behaviours, not as raising test scores.

### Gaps
- Niu, Behar-Horenstein & Garvan (2013, *Educational Research Review*) was not retrieved. From memory it is a college-level meta-analysis with a small mean effect (~0.2), but this is unverified, so do not cite figures.
- Abrami 2015 moderator results by school level (elementary vs secondary) were not retrieved.
- The randomized COR trial's sample, effect size and year were not confirmed (likely Wineburg et al., 2022, JEP).
- Piagetian formal-operational / hypothetico-deductive onset (~11–12+) evidence was not searched in this pass.

## 4. Automated / LLM scoring of critical thinking and argumentation

### Takeaway
LLM agreement with human raters on student writing is **moderate and very uneven**: QWK or correlation mostly 0.30–0.80 across 65 studies. It approaches human-level agreement only with a rubric-anchored prompt, calibrated exemplars, self-consistency and human oversight. In one middle and high school study, kappa was 0.73–0.75 with those supports and fell to about 0 without them. Scoring of CT subskills is still exploratory.

### Cited Findings
- A PRISMA review of 65 studies (Jan 2022 to Aug 2025) found LLM-human agreement (QWK/Pearson/Spearman) mostly 0.30–0.80, with mixed findings and inconsistent reporting. It is an arXiv preprint. — [arXiv 2512.14561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14561)
- A critical scoping review of 46 studies (2022–2025) on LLM argumentative-essay assessment found the field fragmented and poorly grounded in argumentation theory. — [Favero et al. 2026, ELLIS Alicante](https://ellisalicante.org/publications/favero2026aaesreview/)
- AERA 2026 abstract: GPT-4o scored middle and high school students' written evaluations of scientific evidence. With a rubric-anchored prompt, exemplar calibration and self-consistency, agreement reached **κ = .731–.754**. Without those supports and without human oversight it **collapsed to κ = −.051**. The workflow cut scoring labour, partly offset by transcription time. — [AERA 2026 program](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/aera/aera26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2284689/) (from a search summary)
- Peczuh, Kumar, Baker, Lan et al. (arXiv 2025, v2 Feb 2026; EDM 2026) built a CT-subskill rubric from an established skills progression for argumentative essays. They compared GPT-5 zero- and few-shot, fine-tuned Llama 3.1 8B and ModernBERT. **Fine-tuned Llama 3.1 8B was best.** All models were weaker on subskills with subtle proficiency distinctions or imbalanced labels, and the authors recommend human validation. — [arXiv 2510.12915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12915); [EDM 2026 proceedings](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2026/proceedings/2026.EDM.full-papers.174/index.html)
- In higher education, several LLMs agreed poorly with humans on short university essays even after averaging three runs. They were worst on interpretive, discipline-expert dimensions. — [arXiv 2508.02442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02442)
- Out-of-the-box LLM grading agreed weakly with humans. LLMs **over-scored short or underdeveloped essays and under-scored long essays with minor errors**, a length bias. — [arXiv 2603.23714](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23714)
- At AIME-Con 2026, LLMs were overconfident and self-consistent, and majority voting did not improve agreement with humans. — [ACL Anthology 2026.aimecon-main.60](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aimecon-main.60.pdf); [2026.aimecon-main.31](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aimecon-main.31.pdf)
- Five LLMs, including Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4, scoring Italian-language college essays showed low, non-significant agreement with humans (Gaggioli et al.). Japanese first-year essays scored by GPT-4/o3-mini were close to the expert on some dimensions but inaccurate on cohesion and coherence. — [Texto Livre (UFMG)](https://periodicos.ufmg.br/index.php/textolivre/article/download/63123/51792); [Yamaguchi Univ. repository](https://ypir.lib.yamaguchi-u.ac.jp/yp/1923/files/169365) (both from a search summary, so attribution of each study to each URL is uncertain)
- Lee et al. 2024 (generalizability theory, AP Chinese writing): human raters were more reliable than ChatGPT, and **hybrid human+AI scoring improved reliability**. A validity caveat (Attali 2007): agreement alone does not show the automated scores measure the same construct. — [UMD MARC](https://education.umd.edu/node/42881); [ACL Anthology AIME-Con](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aimecon-main.60.pdf)
- Contrast: GPT-4 showed high QWK on 600 ASAP argumentative essays, a general essay-quality dataset rather than a CT rubric. — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10524948) (attribution from a search summary)

### Inferences
- Design implications for a WhatsApp tool:
  1. Use a short, behaviourally anchored rubric of 3–4 levels per subskill, like SHEG's Beginning/Emerging/Mastery.
  2. Include calibrated exemplars per level in the prompt.
  3. Use self-consistency, and flag uncertain cases for teacher review.
  4. Control for length bias, since short WhatsApp messages risk systematic mis-scoring.
  5. Validate on a local Spanish-language sample double-scored by teachers (target QWK/κ ≥ 0.7) before relying on it.
- Low-stakes, formative use is defensible. High-stakes use is not supported by 2023–2026 evidence.

### Gaps
- No study found on LLM scoring of CT in Spanish-speaking K-12 students, or on chat-style (WhatsApp) short-form responses.
- QWK values and sample details for arXiv 2510.12915 were not extracted (abstract page only).

## 5. Recommendation: best age band for a phone/WhatsApp tool

### Takeaway
Target **ages 11–14 (Peru: 6º primaria to 3º secundaria; core 1º–3º secundaria)**, with 15-year-olds (4º sec.) as a PISA-comparable anchor. This band has the strongest combined case: metacognitive growth through adolescence, validated constructed-response tasks (SHEG COR has middle school evidence; Cornell X starts at grade 5), brief instruction that produces visible change, writing fluent enough for text-based scoring, and the PISA 15-year-old benchmark where Peru scores below average.

### Cited Findings
- Metacognitive ability improves significantly from 11 to 17 and peaks in late adolescence. — [Weil et al. 2013, PMC3719211](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3719211)
- Self-regulation gains clearly from middle childhood to adolescence, while children's spontaneous monitoring and control are still immature at 7–10. — [Augsburg Schneider review](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/deliver/index/docId/104336/file/104336.pdf); [Bayard et al. 2024](https://boris.unibe.ch/160923/8/cdev.13486.pdf); [O'Leary & Sloutsky](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5397377)
- Cornell Level X starts at grade 5 (about age 10–11). — [Cornell CCTT sheet](https://health.wvu.edu/media/15198/cornell-critical-thinking-tests.pdf)
- Middle schoolers showed large lateral-reading gains after six one-hour COR lessons (3/87 to 67/87 in one class), and 8th graders gained after 90 minutes. — [DIG COR blog](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/blog/teaching-middle-school-students); [Stanford GSE news](https://ed-old.gse.stanford.edu/news/it-doesn-t-take-long-learn-how-spot-misinformation-online-stanford-study-finds)
- Even US high schoolers mostly score "Beginning" on source evaluation (more than 96%), so there is no ceiling effect at 11–17. — [SHEG 2019](https://purl.stanford.edu/cz440cm8408)
- Peru's 15-year-olds scored 23/60 on PISA 2022 creative thinking against an OECD average of 33. PISA 2025 adds SRL and metacognition for 15-year-olds. — [OECD Peru factsheet](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-results-2022-volume-iii-factsheets_041a90f1-en/peru_fb5a37a1-en.html); [Scottish Gov PISA 2025](https://www.gov.scot/publications/pisa-2025-national-report-scotland/pages/8/)
- LLM scoring reaches near-human agreement for middle and high school written evidence evaluation only with rubric anchoring, exemplars and oversight. — [AERA 2026](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/aera/aera26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2284689/)

### Inferences
- Under 10, text-based self-explanation underestimates metacognition and writing fluency limits the signal. Voice notes or teacher mediation would be needed, which hurts feasibility.
- Ages 15–17 are valid, but SHEG data show the same low baseline. Phone access is higher there, but the 11–14 transition offers more instructional leverage, and PISA-age 4º sec. gives a benchmark link.
- Suggested constructs for automatic scoring, all with published K-12 analogues:
  - source evaluation and lateral reading (SHEG COR)
  - claim, evidence and reasoning (argumentation subskills)
  - identifying assumptions (Cornell X)
  - reflection and monitoring on one's own process (PISA LDW SRL; OECD-CERI "reflecting")

### Gaps
- No Peru-specific data found on WhatsApp or phone access by age for students, or on MINEDU's CT competency descriptors (CNEB). Both are worth checking separately.
- No validation study found of any CT instrument with Peruvian students.
