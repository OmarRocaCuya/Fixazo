## 1. PISA 2022: digital distraction in class, score gaps, leisure screen use (OECD and Peru)

### Takeaway
In PISA 2022, digital distraction in math class is common across the OECD (about 30% of students say it happens in most or all lessons) and goes with lower math scores. Peru reports less of it than the OECD average (about 21% for own devices, 19% for peers' devices), probably because more than half of Peruvian 15-year-olds use no devices for leisure at school. In Peru the problem is less the amount of distraction in class and more the very high exposure outside school (see section 4).

### Cited Findings
- OECD average: 30% of students said students in their classes got distracted by digital devices in every or most mathematics lessons. In Argentina, Uruguay and Chile it is more than half; in Japan and Korea it is under 10%. [verified] — [OECD, PISA in Focus 2024/124 "Managing screen time" (May 2024)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/05/managing-screen-time_023f2390/7c225af4-en.pdf)
- Using a wider threshold ("at least some lessons"), 65% of OECD students said they were distracted by their own device use, and 59% by other students' devices. [search summary] — [EdWeek, Dec 2023](https://edweek.org/leadership/digital-distractions-in-class-linked-to-lower-academic-performance/2023/12); [OECD, Students, digital devices and success (2024)](https://oecd.org/en/publications/students-digital-devices-and-success_9e4c0624-en)
- Students distracted by their own devices score about 15 points lower in math on average, which the press describes as about three-quarters of a school year of learning. [search summary; check the exact figure in PISA 2022 Vol II] — [Blu Radio](https://www.bluradio.com/sociedad/educacion/pisa-distraccion-con-celulares-en-clase-equivale-a-perder-tres-cuartas-partes-de-un-curso-academico-cb20); [PISA 2022 Vol II](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-ii_a97db61c-en/full-report/component-10.html)
- **Peru:** 21% of students reported being distracted by their own device use in math lessons (OECD 30%), and 19% by other students' devices (OECD 25%). About 17% said they cannot work well in most or all lessons, and 23% said students don't listen to the teacher. [search summary of the OECD Peru country note; the page returned 403 when fetched] — [OECD PISA 2022 Country Note: Peru](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-i-and-ii-country-notes_ed6fbcc5-en/peru_3e71791c-en.html); [Peru factsheet PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/11/pisa-2022-results-volume-i-and-ii-country-notes_2fca04b9/peru_21d86e8b/3e71791c-en.pdf)
- **Peru:** In Viet Nam, Malta, Japan, Cambodia, Guatemala, Paraguay, Brunei and Peru, more than 50% of students spend **no** time on digital devices for leisure at school. Peru's position in the OECD ranking chart is also consistent with lower-than-average distraction. [verified] — [OECD PISA in Focus 2024/124](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/05/managing-screen-time_023f2390/7c225af4-en.pdf)
- Learning use versus leisure use (OECD averages). [verified] — [same source](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/05/managing-screen-time_023f2390/7c225af4-en.pdf)
  - Spending 1 to 5 hours a day on devices for learning at school goes with about 20 points higher in math than spending none.
  - Spending 5 to 7 hours a day for learning goes with 12 points lower than spending 3 to 5 hours. The OECD calls this the "Goldilocks" pattern.
  - Spending more than 1 hour a day on devices for leisure at school goes with more than 9 points lower in math and a lower sense of belonging.
- Weekday leisure use (OECD averages). [verified] — [same source](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/05/managing-screen-time_023f2390/7c225af4-en.pdf)
  - Three-quarters of students spend more than 1 hour per weekday on social networks. The share is over 80% in Chile, Denmark, Ireland, Italy and Uruguay, and lowest in Panama at 48%.
  - More than 1 hour per weekday on social networks, messaging or browsing for fun goes with 5 to 20 points lower in math than 1 hour or less.
  - Light use (up to 1 hour) scores higher than no use at all.
- Easy distraction, smartphones and notifications (OECD averages). [verified] — [same source](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/05/managing-screen-time_023f2390/7c225af4-en.pdf)
  - 54% of students "agree" they are easily distracted. This is more likely among students using social networks or the internet for fun for more than 1 hour per weekday.
  - 98% of 15-year-olds have a smartphone of their own at home.
  - Students are less distracted when notifications are off, when devices are not open in class, and when they don't feel pressure to answer messages.
- Phone policies (OECD analysis). [verified] — [same source](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/05/managing-screen-time_023f2390/7c225af4-en.pdf)
  - School device guidelines "show little association" with the likelihood of distraction.
  - Bans "may reduce distractions" but are "not always effectively enforced".
  - Where phones are banned at school, students in some countries are less likely to turn off notifications at bedtime.
  - In several systems, bans are more common in lower-performing schools.
  - Higher ICT self-competence, ICT teacher training and family support all go with less distraction.

### Inferences
- PISA does not suggest Peru has an unusual problem with distraction inside the classroom. Peru is below the OECD average, likely because of limited device access in class and lower device use at school. The bigger risk in Peru is the volume of use outside school (section 4) and how fast access is growing.
- All PISA associations are cross-sectional and cannot show cause. The OECD adjusts for socio-economic status, but that does not establish causality.

### Gaps
- I could not open Peru's exact percentages in the primary country note (HTTP 403). The figures of 21% and 19% come from a search summary and should be confirmed in the PDF or in the PIF_124 Excel (https://webfs.oecd.org/pisa/PIF_124_Figures_Tables.xlsx).
- I found no Peru-specific score gap for distracted students, and no Peru figure for leisure hours on weekdays.
- I did not find MINEDU-UMC's national PISA 2022 report on digital distraction.

## 2. School smartphone bans: who banned, measured effects, and Peru's legal status

### Takeaway
More than half of the world's education systems now ban phones in school: 114 systems, or 58%, as of March 2026. The evidence on effects is mixed.
- **Norway** (quasi-experimental): gains for girls in mental-health care use, grades and bullying, but critics dispute how robust they are.
- **England** (SMART Schools, cross-sectional): no difference in wellbeing or attainment between restrictive and permissive schools.

**Peru has a law.** Ley N.° 32385 (2025) restricts phone use in class in basic education. Its reglamento is DS N.° 018-2026-MINEDU (primaria and secundaria). RM N.° 612-2026-MINEDU (October 2026) extends the restriction to educación inicial.

### Cited Findings

  | Date | Education systems with a national ban | Share of countries |
  |---|---|---|
  | June 2023 | — | 24% |
  | End of 2023 | 60 | — |
  | End of 2024 | 79 | ~40% |
  | March 2026 | 114 | 58% |

- **Norway (Abrahamsson, NHH Discussion Paper 2024; later published in Journal of Human Resources).** [search summary] — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/hhsnhheco/2024_5f001.htm); [JHR](https://jhr.uwpress.org/node/16076); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/04/27/metro/norway-study-smartphones-banned-in-schools)
  - Design: middle-school bans studied with administrative data.
  - After a ban, girls' consultations for psychological symptoms fell.
  - Bullying fell for both boys and girls.
  - Girls' GPA rose, and so did their probability of entering the academic high-school track.
  - Effects were larger for girls from low-income backgrounds.
- **Critiques of the Norway study.** [search summary] — [Grimoire Manor](https://grimoiremanor.substack.com/p/did-a-new-study-show-that-a-norwegian-); [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2024/04/the-norwegian-ban-on-smart-phones-in-middle-schools.html)
  - The mental-health effect shows up in specialist visits, not GP visits.
  - The bullying estimates are only marginally significant.
  - Grade gains are modest.
- **England, SMART Schools (Goodyear et al., Lancet Regional Health – Europe, e-pub 4 Feb 2025).** [search summary] — [Univ. of Birmingham publication page](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/school-phone-policies-and-their-association-with-mental-wellbeing/); [SMART Schools project](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/research/projects/smart-schools); [newsletter](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/documents/college-les/sportex/smart-schools/smart-schools-study-newsletter-issue-2.pdf)
  - Design: cross-sectional; 1,227 pupils in 30 secondary schools (20 restrictive, 10 permissive).
  - No meaningful difference in WEMWBS wellbeing, anxiety, depression, physical activity, sleep or attainment.
  - Restrictive policies only slightly reduced phone use.
  - Across all pupils, higher phone and social-media time was associated with worse mental health.
- **Brazil:** Lei 15.100 of 13 January 2025 restricts student use of portable electronic devices in public and private basic-education schools, including recess, with pedagogical, accessibility and health exceptions. [background, not fetched] — [Planalto, Lei 15.100/2025](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/lei/L15100.htm). I found no outcome evaluation.
- **Peru, the law.** [search summary] — [Gestión](https://gestion.pe/peru/uso-de-celulares-en-los-colegios-que-estara-prohibido-y-permitido-para-los-estudiantes-noticia/); [El Peruano, "Uso del celular en colegios: restricciones y excepciones"](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/288185-uso-del-celular-en-colegios-restricciones-y-excepciones); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/tecnologia/peru-oficializa-una-ley-que-regula-el-uso-de-celulares-en-todos-los-colegios-del-pais-632889)
  - Ley N.° 32385 (2025) regulates phone use in all basic-education institutions and programs.
  - It restricts use during class in primaria and secundaria, public and private.

  - Directors must set protocols, supervise and apply corrective measures following principles of gradualness and proportionality, and post visible notices.
  - Stated aims: academic performance, cyberbullying, and overexposure to the internet.
- **Peru, the draft reglamento** (RM 052-2026-MINEDU, late January 2026, open for public comment). [search summary] — [Gestión](https://gestion.pe/peru/uso-de-celulares-en-los-colegios-que-estara-prohibido-y-permitido-para-los-estudiantes-noticia/); [Exitosa PDF](https://statics.exitosanoticias.pe/2026/01/697b8bc93603f.pdf?MM=175457)

[verified via El Peruano news; DS date not stated] — [El Peruano, 11 Oct 2026](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/306590-minedu-restringe-el-uso-de-celulares-durante-jornada-escolar-de-la-educacion-inicial)

This covers calls, messaging, games, social media, photos and video. Exceptions: educational needs, learning barriers, health or disability. [verified] — [El Peruano](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/306590-minedu-restringe-el-uso-de-celulares-durante-jornada-escolar-de-la-educacion-inicial)
- **Peru, oversight:** A related MINEDU resolution dated about 3 October 2026 (RM 598-2026-MINEDU) assigns DRE/UGEL oversight of compliance. [search summary; exact content not confirmed] — [LP Derecho PDF](https://img.lpderecho.pe/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Resolucion-Ministerial-598-2026-MINEDU-LPDerecho.pdf)
- **Peru, earlier bills:**
  - PL 5532/2022-CR (Milagros Jáuregui, July 2023) banned smartphones during class from inicial to secundaria. [verified] — [Congreso](https://comunicaciones.congreso.gob.pe/damos-cuenta/congreso-prohibe-el-uso-de-celulares-en-colegios)
  - PL 10472/2024-CR (Katy Ugarte) gave MINEDU 90 days to issue the regulation.

[search summary] — [La República, Jan 2025](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2025/01/16/escolares-en-peru-ya-no-podrian-llevar-celulares-a-clase-tras-nueva-propuesta-del-congreso-que-evalua-prohibirlos-166624); [Expreso](https://www.expreso.com.pe/politica/congresista-luis-kamiche-propone-ley-para-prohibir-uso-de-celulares-en-colegios-del-peru-nueva-zelanda-noticia/1171464/)

### Inferences
- The strongest causal-style evidence (Norway) suggests modest benefits, concentrated among girls and disadvantaged students.
- Cross-sectional English data and the PISA analysis suggest that the policy label matters less than enforcement and design.
- For Peru, implementation through DRE/UGEL oversight and school protocols, along with digital-skills education, will matter more than the law itself.
- Peru's PISA baseline of in-class distraction (about 20%) is already below the OECD average. Measured classroom gains from the ban may therefore be smaller than in Chile, Argentina or Uruguay.

## 3. Cognitive load and multitasking: does information excess hurt learning?

### Takeaway
The experimental and meta-analytic literature consistently finds that multitasking and media distraction during study goes with lower performance. Cognitive load theory explains why: working memory is limited, and redundant or split sources consume it. The links are reliable but small to moderate (r ≈ −0.25), and the evidence comes mostly from university students.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta-analysis (2025), academic media multitasking and achievement.** [search summary] — [Discover Education, Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-025-01028-x)
  - 11 correlational studies, weighted mean r = −0.25 (described as "low in intensity but consistent").
  - Research on younger school levels is sparse.
- **Review:** media multitasking interferes with attention and working memory. It is associated with lower GPA, test performance, recall, reading comprehension and note-taking, both in lectures and during study. [search summary] — [Univ. of Alabama repository, review](https://ir.ua.edu/handle/123456789/7949)
- **Texting:** more in-class texting predicts lower final grades. Classroom policy can reduce the harm but not eliminate it. [search summary] — [UWL Press, New Vistas](https://uwlpress.uwl.ac.uk/newvistas/article/20/galley/20/download)
- **Sana, Weston & Cepeda (2013):** laptop multitasking lowered comprehension for the multitasker and also for nearby peers who could see the screen. [background, not fetched] — [Computers & Education, doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2012.10.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2012.10.003)
- **Sweller (1988), cognitive load theory:** working memory is limited, so instructional designs that impose extraneous load impair schema acquisition. The split-attention and redundancy effects follow from this. [background, not fetched] — [Cognitive Science, doi:10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4)
- **Ward et al. (2017), "Brain Drain":** the mere presence of one's own smartphone reduced available cognitive capacity. Later replications were mixed. [background, not fetched] — [J. Assoc. Consumer Research, doi:10.1086/691462](https://doi.org/10.1086/691462)
- **PISA:** in OECD averages, notifications on and pressure to respond go with more distraction. [verified] — [OECD PISA in Focus 124](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/05/managing-screen-time_023f2390/7c225af4-en.pdf)

### Inferences
- The mechanism (limited working memory plus task-switching costs) is well supported. The claim that "too many stimuli harms learning" is better supported for in-the-moment task performance than for long-term effects on attention capacity.

### Gaps
- I found no Peruvian experimental studies on multitasking or cognitive load in school students.
- I did not verify Ward 2017 replication outcomes or the size of the split-attention effect in this session.

## 4. Peru: connectivity, screen and social-media use, mental health, cyberbullying

### Takeaway
Internet use among Peruvian children is growing quickly and runs mainly through phones:
- 80.9% of children aged 6–17 used the internet in Q2 2026, including 92.1% of those aged 12–17.
- 64.9% connect through a cell phone.
- A 2025 Concytec-funded survey found adolescents average about 4 hours a day on social media, and 8 in 10 start before age 13.

## 5. Haidt's "The Anxious Generation" versus critics: what the evidence supports

### Takeaway
On correlation, both sides agree. Heavy social-media and phone use is associated with worse adolescent mental health, but average effect sizes are small. What remains contested is causation and whether smartphones drove the post-2012 rise. Experimental reduction studies are mixed: Ferguson's 2025 meta-analysis finds no overall effect, while Haidt and Rausch dispute his methods. The evidence supports moderate, targeted restrictions more than claims of a single cause.

### Cited Findings
- **Odgers (Nature review, 2024)** argues the claim that social media caused an epidemic is not supported. Research finds a mix of no, small and mixed associations, and blaming social media may distract from the real causes. [search summary] — [Platformer](https://platformer.news/anxious-generation-jonathan-haidt-debate-critique/); [Sherwood](https://sherwood.news/tech/inside-the-debate-over-jonathan-haidts-the-anxious-generation/); original: [Nature, doi:10.1038/d41586-024-00902-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/d41586-024-00902-2) [background, not fetched]
- **Orben (2020)** reviewed 80 systematic reviews and meta-analyses. She found a small negative association between digital technology and adolescent wellbeing, with unclear direction and confounding. [search summary] — [Platformer](https://platformer.news/anxious-generation-jonathan-haidt-debate-critique/); original [Soc Psychiatry Psychiatr Epidemiol, doi:10.1007/s00127-019-01825-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00127-019-01825-4) [background, not fetched]
- **Valkenburg et al. (2022)** meta-analysed 226 studies and found an effect size of about zero for social-media use and overall wellbeing. [search summary] — [Holden Thorp Substack](https://holdenthorp.substack.com/p/more-on-the-muddled-science-on-teens)
- **Ferguson (2025)**, a meta-analysis of social-media reduction experiments, found no overall effect on mental health. Haidt and Rausch contest his methods. [search summary] — [Platformer](https://platformer.news/social-media-bans-candice-odgers-haidt); [IEA](https://iea.org.uk/media/evidence-lacking-for-sweeping-social-media-panic/)

### Inferences
- A defensible position for Peru: phone restrictions in class are a low-cost measure. They are backed by attention and learning evidence, and modest wellbeing gains are plausible for some groups. They should not be expected to solve adolescent mental health on their own.
