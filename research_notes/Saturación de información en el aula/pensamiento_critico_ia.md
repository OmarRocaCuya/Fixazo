# Students' sense-making (critical thinking, source evaluation, misinformation) and generative AI — Peru focus, to Oct 2026

Note on method: ~15 web searches; several figures come from press or secondary summaries rather than primary reports. These are flagged inline. Verify the flagged figures against the primary PDFs before publishing.

## 1. PISA: fact vs opinion (2018), creative thinking (2022), PISA 2025

### Takeaway
Only about 1% of Peruvian 15-year-olds reached PISA 2018 reading Levels 5–6, the levels at which students can tell fact from opinion using implicit cues. The OECD average was about 9%. Peru scored 23/60 in PISA 2022 creative thinking (OECD 33). In PISA 2025, released 8 Sep 2026, Peru's reading fell to 390, below its 2015 level.

### Cited findings
- **PISA 2018 (older, labelled).** Students at reading Levels 5–6 "can establish distinctions between fact and opinion, based on implicit cues pertaining to the content or source of the information." Peru had about 1% of students at these levels; the OECD average was about 9%. — [OECD PISA 2018 Country Note Peru](https://search.oecd.org/pisa/publications/PISA2018_CN_PER.pdf)
- **PISA 2018:** about 46% of Peruvian students reached at least Level 2 (baseline) in reading; the OECD average was 77%. — [OECD PISA 2018 Country Note Peru](https://search.oecd.org/pisa/publications/PISA2018_CN_PER.pdf)
- **PISA 2018 headline:** fewer than 1 in 10 students across the OECD could tell fact from opinion. This is the "9%" figure widely cited in the press. — [Quartz](https://qz.com/1759474/only-9-percent-of-15-year-olds-can-distinguish-between-fact-and-opinion)
- **PISA 2018 Latin America regional analysis (TUM):** [PISA 2018 Peru summary, TUM](https://www.pisa.tum.de/fileadmin/w00bgi/www/Berichtsbaende_und_Zusammenfassungungen/PISA_2018_Peru_final_17_03_2020.pdf); [PISA 2018 LatAm, TUM](https://www.pisa.tum.de/fileadmin/w00bgi/www/Berichtsbaende_und_Zusammenfassungungen/PISA_2018_Latam_final.pdf)
- **PISA 2022 creative thinking:** Peru took part. It scored 23 out of 60, significantly below the OECD average of 33, and level with Brazil, Panama, El Salvador and Saudi Arabia. Singapore led with 41; Canada and Korea scored 38. — [OECD PISA 2022 Vol. III factsheet, Peru](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-results-2022-volume-iii-factsheets_041a90f1-en/peru_fb5a37a1-en.html) (the page returned 403 to my fetch, so the figures come via the search snippet); [CUHK annex with the global table](https://cpr.cuhk.edu.hk/wp-content/uploads/newscentre/pressrelease/20240618-PISA-2022-Creative-Thinking_Annex_Eng.pdf)
- **Peruvian press on creative thinking:** Peru ranked 7th of 12 Latin American systems, and 47th of 64 overall. — [Gestión](https://gestion.pe/peru/un-futuro-sin-ideas-peru-se-queda-atras-en-creatividad-segun-evaluacion-pisa-peru-prueba-pisa-america-latina-ocde-escolares-estudiantes-noticia/); [La República](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2024/06/18/peru-obtuvo-bajo-puntaje-en-pensamiento-creativo-1237194); [Canal N](https://canaln.pe/actualidad/prueba-pisa-peru-debajo-promedio-pensamiento-creativo-n474356)
  - A snippet claimed "only 48% scored above level 3". This is unverified and likely garbled. Do not use it without the OECD factsheet.
- **PISA 2025 release:** the OECD launched PISA 2025 results on 8 September 2026. — [OECD, SG speech at the PISA 2025 launch](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/speech-statements/2026/09/international-launch-of-pisa-2025.html)
- **PISA 2025 innovative domain, "Learning in the Digital World":** tested for the first time. Deeper analysis is due in 2027. The OECD reported that using devices for learning at school goes with better science results only up to a point; results start to fall beyond about 5 hours a day. — [OECD launch speech](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/speech-statements/2026/09/international-launch-of-pisa-2025.html); [NZPF summary](https://www.nzpf.ac.nz/hail-articles/1VJNF1d/); [K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/pisa-test-digital-learning-2025/689881/)
- **Peru in PISA 2025 (press, Sep 2026):**
  - Reading 390, against 408 in 2022 and 398 in 2015 (–18 points since 2022).
  - Science 406, mathematics 382.
  - OECD averages: science 484, reading 463, mathematics 465. Some outlets give 461 for reading, so verify.
  - 42% of Peruvian students reached baseline reading, against 62% in Chile and 69% across the OECD.
  - Sample: 6,991 students in 260 schools.
  - Sources: [Gestión](https://gestion.pe/economia/pisa-2025-peru-se-ubica-incluso-por-debajo-de-hace-una-decada-los-pasos-hacia-atras-que-llevaron-a-este-resultado-noticia/); [Panamericana](https://panamericana.pe/locales/473990-peru-retrocede-pisa-2025-resultados-vuelven-niveles-decada); [América TV](https://www.americatv.com.pe/noticias/actualidad/peru-retrocede-niveles-hace-decada-informe-pisa-2025-n522431); [La Cámara magazine PDF](https://lacamara.pe/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Revista-1247-END01.pdf)
  - Conflict: an Andina piece headlined "Perú demuestra avances" with "9,000 escolares" appears to predate the release or use a different framing. [Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-pisa-2025-peru-demuestra-avances-aprendizajes-participacion-9000-escolares-1041960.aspx)

### Inferences
- Across three PISA cycles the pattern is consistent. Peru's shortfall is largest in higher-order skills: evaluating sources, telling fact from opinion, generating ideas. The 2025 reading decline suggests the gap is widening, not closing, during the generative-AI years. This is correlation only; COVID after-effects and policy changes are confounders.

### Gaps
- I found no Peru-specific PISA 2018 percentage on the single fact/opinion item. The ~1% Level 5–6 share is a proxy. The OECD "21st-Century Readers" (2021) report has item-level data; I did not fetch it.
- Peru's PISA 2025 "Learning in the Digital World" score was not found, and may not be released until 2027.
- The PISA 2025 figures above come from press coverage, not the OECD country note.

## 2. ENLA / UMC (Peru): higher-order vs literal reading

### Takeaway
National assessments show that most Peruvian students fail to reach "satisfactory" reading. Only about 1 in 3 fourth graders (2024) and about 1 in 5 second-year secondary students (2023) do.

### Cited findings
- **ENLA 2024, 4th grade primary:** 32.8% satisfactory in reading comprehension. Loreto was lowest at 12.8%. The average score rose 20 points over 2023. — [La República, Apr 2025](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2025/04/14/minedu-revela-que-solo-3-de-cada-10-escolares-de-cuarto-de-primaria-entiende-lo-que-lee-brecha-se-intensifica-en-regiones-amazonicas-746998)
- **ENLA 2024, 6th grade:** regional example, Ayacucho at 22.9% satisfactory in reading. — [DRE Ayacucho press note](https://dreayacucho.gob.pe/storage/documentos/2025/notas-de-prensa/01JRBPQRKTPFWJY6YPVBZ2MQMS.pdf)
- **ENLA 2023, 2nd year secondary:** 18.4% satisfactory in reading, against 20% in 2022. This comes from an opinion column, so verify against the UMC report. — [Diario Correo column](https://diariocorreo.pe/opinion/el-optimismo-una-columna-de-maria-isabel-leon-noticia/); official sources: [ENLA 2023 presentation (Lampadia mirror)](https://www.lampadia.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Presentacion_de_logros_de_aprendizaje_ENLA_2023.pdf), [ENLA 2023 executive summary, Minedu repository](https://repositorio.minedu.gob.pe/handle/20.500.12799/10420)
- **ENLA 2025:** 2nd-year secondary results are stable relative to 2023 in reading and mathematics, with a significant change in urban schools at the satisfactory level. — [Mesa de Concertación working-group document, Jul 2026](https://intranet.mesadeconcertacion.org.pe/storage/documentos/2026-07-30/reunion-del-grupo-de-trabajo-nacional-de-educacion.pdf); [Caretas](https://caretas.pe/?p=806486)

### Inferences
- "Satisfactory" in the ENLA standards implies inferential and critical reading. The 80% or more of secondary students below that level are, by construction, mostly limited to literal or local-inference tasks.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a breakdown by capacity (literal vs inferential vs critical-reflective items, % correct). The UMC "Informe para docentes" or ENLA technical reports at umc.minedu.gob.pe usually include item-level examples. Not fetched.

## 3. Student use of generative AI (Peru / LatAm) and teacher perceptions

### Takeaway
Peruvian university data show that about 73% use AI for assignments. Survey data for school students and on copying is thin and comes from secondary reporting.

### Cited findings
- **UCSS study, 2024, nine Peruvian universities:** 73.2% of students use AI tools to do tasks and assignments. ChatGPT is preferred by 71.3%. Ecuador and Chile are reported above 80%. 99% said they would follow ethical guidelines set by educators. — [La República, Aug 2024](https://larepublica.pe/tecnologia/smartphone/2024/08/24/cuales-son-las-ia-que-prefieren-los-universitarios-peruanos-investigacion-revela-insolito-resultado-1400376); [Panamericana](https://panamericana.pe/tecnologia/420255-inteligencia-artificial-educacion-preferencias-usos-universitarios-peruanos)
- **Young people in Lima:** 75% use AI tools habitually, but only 12.75% feel very prepared for an AI work environment. The date and methodology are unclear. — [Revista Economía](https://www.revistaeconomia.com/?p=124078)
- **Peruvian school students:** an opinion piece cites UCSS data that 67–70% of school students use AI for homework. The methodology is not given, so treat this as unverified. — [KCH Comunicación](https://kchcomunicacion.com/?p=493724)
- **Teachers (Neurometrics/Edured survey, more than 1,000 basic-education teachers):** almost 90% want to learn to use AI, and half cite a lack of connectivity infrastructure. — [ComputerWeekly ES](https://www.computerweekly.com/es/cronica/Nueve-de-cada-10-docentes-peruanos-desea-aprender-a-usar-la-IA)
- 84% of Peruvians surveyed consider it important to incorporate AI in education. — [Mercado Negro](https://www.mercadonegro.pe/actualidad/peru-84-cree-importante-incorporar-ia-en-la-educacion)
- Academic studies on Peruvian university AI use: [Revista Horizontes](https://revistahorizontes.org/index.php/revistahorizontes/article/view/2032/3345); [LACCEI 2025](https://laccei.org/LACCEI2025-Mexico/full-papers/Contribution_737_final_a.pdf); [El Búho, "Los amigos del ChatGPT"](https://elbuho.pe/?p=323476)

### Inferences
- High use (about 70–75%), combined with low baseline evaluation skills (Section 1), is the core of the "access without sense-making" gap. This is inferred, not measured jointly.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous Peru survey of school (secondary) students' AI use, and no figure on the share who copy AI output verbatim.
- I found no survey of Peruvian teachers' perceptions of AI cheating. Candidates for the report writer to note as missing: IPSOS Peru, Minedu, UNESCO/IIPE, Fundación Telefónica.

## 4. Cognitive offloading: experimental and survey evidence

### Takeaway
Unrestricted AI use improves performance while access lasts, but can harm learning once access is removed. Bastani: –17% on the exam. Guardrailed or Socratic tutors avoid the harm, and well-designed, teacher-mediated tutors produce large gains: Nigeria about 0.3 SD; Harvard 0.73–1.3 SD, disputed.

### Cited findings
- **Bastani et al., PNAS 122(26), July 2025.** Field experiment with about 1,000 Turkish high-school math students.
  - During practice, GPT Base raised grades by 48% and GPT Tutor (hints, no direct answers) by 127%.
  - On the unassisted exam, GPT Base students scored 17% lower than controls. GPT Tutor students were roughly equal to controls, so there was no harm and no gain.
  - The mechanism: students used the tool as a "crutch".
  - Sources: [IDEAS/RePEc PNAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html); [SSRN preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486); [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)
- **MIT Media Lab, Kosmyna et al., "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (June 2025, arXiv preprint, not peer-reviewed).**
  - 54 participants aged 18–39, Boston area; EEG during essay writing in three groups: LLM, search engine, brain-only.
  - The LLM group showed the weakest brain connectivity.
  - 83% of the ChatGPT group could not quote a sentence from the essay they had just written, against about 11% in the other groups.
  - The authors published an FAQ asking media not to use words like "dumb" or "brain rot".
  - The "47% drop in brain activity" figure circulating online is unconfirmed.
  - Sources: [Make Tech Easier](https://maketecheasier.com/mit-researchers-wired-eeg-sensors-onto-54-people-writing-essays-and-found-that-the-group-using-chatgpt-not-only-thought-the-least-measured-by-brain-connectivity-but-afterwards-mostly-could-not-recal/); [AVID Open Access](https://avidopenaccess.org/resource/the-cognitive-cost-of-ai-and-convenience-an-mit-study/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/study-reveals-concerning-cognitive-effects-of-using-chatgpt-for-essay-writing/)
- **Lee et al. (Microsoft Research + Carnegie Mellon), CHI 2025, "The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking."**
  - Survey of 319 knowledge workers covering 936 real use examples.
  - Higher confidence in GenAI was associated with less critical thinking; higher self-confidence with more.
  - GenAI shifts critical thinking toward verification, response integration and "task stewardship".
  - Workers reported less effort on routine, low-stakes, time-pressured tasks.
  - These are self-reported associations, not causal findings.
  - Sources: [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1135061); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/11/microsoft_study_ai_critical_thinking/); [Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/Articles/2025/02/21/Study-Generative-AI-Could-Inhibit-Critical-Thinking.aspx)
- **World Bank, Nigeria (Edo State, Benin City), "From Chalkboards to Chatbots", Policy Research WP 11125 (May 2025).**
  - RCT of a 6-week after-school English program using GPT-4 via Microsoft Copilot with teacher guidance, for first-year secondary students.
  - Effect of about 0.3 SD (0.29) on pen-and-paper tests, described as 1.5–2 years of business-as-usual learning.
  - Students also gained on end-of-year exams, AI knowledge and digital skills.
  - Girls gained most.
  - Caveats: short duration; teacher involvement was likely crucial.
  - Sources: [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria); [World Bank working paper PDF](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/cd9cca5b-2ffd-4be1-8313-0f4d2ed6ef4f/download); [World Bank Let's Talk Development](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/addressing-the-learning-crisis-with-generative-ai--lessons-from-); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-tutoring-helps-nigerian-students-gain-two-years-of-learning-in-six-weeks/)
- **Kestin et al., Scientific Reports (June 2025), Harvard Physical Sciences 2.**
  - Randomized crossover design with 194 students, comparing a custom AI tutor built on pedagogical best practices (scaffolded, Socratic-style, did not simply give answers) against in-class active learning.
  - Median post-test 4.5 vs 3.5. Effect size reported as 0.73–1.3 SD via quantile regression. Median time 49 vs 60 minutes.
  - Limits: one topic, two weeks, elite sample, no retention or transfer measure.
  - A January 2026 independent re-analysis found only about 63% completed both conditions and estimated a much smaller advantage (about 16%).
  - Sources: [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/); [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/09/professor-tailored-ai-tutor-to-physics-course-engagement-doubled/); [ETC Journal review](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/); [Clay Ford re-analysis](https://clayford.net/posts/ai_tutoring_nature/index.html)

### Inferences
- Design matters more than access. The same model (GPT-4) harmed learning when it gave answers (Bastani GPT Base). It was neutral with guardrails (GPT Tutor), and positive when embedded in a teacher-led, scaffolded program (Nigeria, Harvard).
- The policy-relevant variable is whether the tool does the cognitive work or prompts the student to do it.

### Gaps
- Tutor CoPilot (Stanford, Wang et al. 2024) was not searched, so no effect sizes are given for it.
- I found no RCT of AI tutors in Peru or the Andean region.
- No study measures directly whether AI use changes source-evaluation or misinformation-detection skills in school students.

## 5. Misinformation exposure of Peruvian youth and media literacy

### Takeaway
Peru is among the most TikTok-dependent news markets in the Spanish-speaking world. Social media is the main news source for about half of under-35s. I found no data on systematic media-literacy instruction in Peruvian schools.

### Cited findings
- **Reuters Digital News Report 2025, Peru.**
  - News sources: Facebook 52%, YouTube 34%, TikTok 33%.
  - TikTok reaches 57% of Peruvians for any purpose.
  - Peru and Colombia are the Spanish-speaking countries that use TikTok most for news.
  - Sources: [El Tiempo (Peru)](https://eltiempo.pe/tendencias/peru-es-el-pais-hispanohablante-que-mas-usa-tiktok-para-revisar-noticias/); [La República, Jun 2025](https://larepublica.pe/tendencias/2025/06/20/peru-se-destaca-como-el-pais-hispanohablante-que-mas-utiliza-tiktok-para-acceder-a-noticias-de-la-actualidad-evat-795960); [Reuters Institute DNR 2025 (ES)](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/es/digital-news-report/2025)
- **DNR 2025, global:** 59% are worried about telling real from fake online. Influencers and video are flagged as misinformation vectors. — [LatAm Journalism Review](https://latamjournalismreview.org/articles/using-facebook-for-the-news-in-decline-and-concerns-grow-over-ais-influence-on-misinformation-reuters-institute-report/); [DPL News](https://dplnews.com/?p=278910)
- **DNR 2026, Peru:**
  - Social networks are the main news source for 49% of under-35s.
  - Facebook remains first for news, followed by TikTok.
  - The report highlights about 6.7 million voters aged 18–29, a group highly exposed to digital content ahead of the 2026 elections.
  - Sources: [El Peruano](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/299126-dia-mundial-de-las-redes-sociales-por-que-se-celebra-cada-30-de-junio); [Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-dia-mundial-las-redes-sociales-por-se-celebra-cada-30-junio-899271.aspx)
- **DNR 2026, global:**
  - Social and video platforms overtook news sites and TV as the main gateway to news for the first time.
  - Including AI chatbots, third-party access reaches 56%.
  - Trust in news is at 37%, the lowest since 2015.
  - Among 18–24s, more than half get news mainly from social and video platforms.
  - Sources: [Señal News, Jun 2026](https://senalnews.com/es/data/las-plataformas-digitales-superan-a-los-medios-tradicionales-en-consumo-de-noticias); [Observador CR](https://observador.cr/los-videos-cortos-se-convierten-en-principal-fuente-de-informacion-de-jovenes-alerta-un-informe-del-instituto-reuters/)

### Inferences
- Exposure (TikTok and social media as primary news sources), combined with low evaluation skill (about 1% at the PISA fact/opinion level, 42% below baseline reading in 2025), suggests that a large share of Peruvian youth consume news in formats they lack the skills to evaluate.

### Gaps
- I found no Peru-specific DNR figure for misinformation concern, and no 2026 Peru TikTok percentage. A 27% figure is from 2024 (AFP) and should not be presented as current.
- I found no IPSOS Peru youth misinformation survey in this pass.
- I found no evidence on media-literacy content in Peru's Currículo Nacional (2016) or on Minedu media-literacy programs. Not searched in depth; flag as a gap rather than "absent".
